[Discover](https://venice.ai/discover), in the Venice app, puts you, a friend or a pet into someone else's video from one photo each. Every one of its 54 video templates is a Seedance 2.5 request you can send yourself. This guide shows how the templates are built, what goes wrong when you swap someone into a clip, and the requests that do it.

## Two ways to swap someone in

Discover's templates use one of two Seedance 2.5 models, and so can you:

| | Seedance 2.5 Edit | Seedance 2.5 Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Model | `seedance-2-5-edit-video-basic` | `seedance-2-5-reference-to-video-basic` |
| What it does | Edits the clip itself: your photo replaces the person, and the rest of the frame stays | Renders the clip again from a version of it with nobody in it, so only your photos give the people their look |
| You send | The clip, and a photo per person | A clay version of the clip, a photo per person, and an image of the empty scene |
| Good for | Quick swaps, and clips where a hand comes close to the camera | Swaps that have to replace a face, hair and clothes completely, and two or three people at once |
| Discover templates | 21 | 33 |

Both take a source clip between 4 and 30 seconds, and both bill by the second of output. Start with Edit. Move to Reference when the original person keeps showing through.

## How a Reference swap is built

The common failure is that the model keeps the person who was already in the clip. A Reference template removes everything the model could copy, one step at a time:

<div className="vg">
<ol className="vg-flow">
  <li><b>Trim the clip</b><code>ffmpeg</code><span>Four to 30 seconds at 24 fps, with a frame count of 8n + 1, so the output keeps every frame.</span></li>
  <li><b>Make the people clay</b><code>Seedance 2.5 Edit</code><span>Every figure you'll replace becomes a smooth, bald clay figure. Their face, hair and clothes are gone.</span></li>
  <li><b>Empty the set</b><code>/image/multi-edit</code><span>Remove the people from one frame. That plate gives the render the room, the light and the colours.</span></li>
  <li><b>Render</b><code>Seedance 2.5 Reference</code><span>The clay clip carries the motion, the plate the scene, and your photos the people.</span></li>
</ol>
</div>

Discover adds one step outside Venice: it turns the clay clip into a greyscale depth video, so not even the clay surface reaches the render. The clay clip is the part you make with the Venice API, and it already carries every movement, cut and camera angle.

<Accordion title="The Hotel Lobby prompt, lightly shortened">
**Goal.** Generate a video of two performers side by side under a hanging microphone in a plain orange studio, the left one singing and the right one reacting, re-rendered shot by shot from @Video1.

**Assets.** @Video1 is a fine-blockout reference. Preserve its five shots and every hard cut, the camera position and framing in each shot, both figures' positions, depth and sizes in the frame, every gesture, arm, hand and head movement, the left figure's singing mouth and the right figure's closed mouth, occlusion, the hanging microphone, and the music in its audio track; do not adopt its greyscale blockout look, its placeholder figures or its dark background. @Video1 shows only depth, so the figures are identified by position: the figure on the left of the frame in every shot is the left performer, and the figure on the right of the frame is the right performer. @Image1 is used for the left performer's face or head, hair, fur or skin, build and clothing, and @Image2 for the right performer's; do not use the image backgrounds or any text in them. @Image3 is an empty set plate of the scene: use it only for the plain orange backdrop, the floor, the hanging microphone, the light and the colour grade, not as a frame, a pose or a timing. It contains no people: every person, face, head and body comes only from @Image1, @Image2 and the placeholder figures in @Video1.

**Subjects.** There are exactly two performers in every shot: the left performer always looks only like @Image1 and the right performer only like @Image2; they are separate people and never swap sides, faces or clothes, in any shot, after any cut or when the right one turns their back to the camera. Only the left performer sings the vocals of the song in the audio track of @Video1, in perfect lip sync, word for word. The right performer never sings or mouths the words: their lips stay closed; they listen, look at the left performer and gesture along. Glasses only if they are in the photo of that performer.

**Scene.** A plain orange studio backdrop with a light floor and a microphone hanging from above between the two performers, as in the set plate.

**Events.** Shot 1 (0 to 1.8 s): medium shot from the head to the knees, the left performer from @Image1 on the left and the right performer from @Image2 on the right. Shot 2 (1.8 to 4.4 s): wide full-body shot. Shot 3 (4.4 to 7.8 s): medium shot from the head to the thighs. Shot 4 (7.8 to 11.6 s): wide full-body shot, where the right performer turns their back to the camera for a moment and stays on the right. Shot 5 (11.6 s to the end): medium shot from the head to the knees. The left performer is absorbed in the song, singing with feeling, eyes often closed. The right performer is cool and amused, enjoying the song with a closed mouth. Rerender the left placeholder figure as the performer from @Image1, the right one as the performer from @Image2, and the scene as the orange studio from the set plate.

**Consistency.** Keep both performers' faces, hair and clothing identical to their own photos, the left one always on the left and the right one always on the right, and the studio identical to the set plate in every shot.
</Accordion>

Images are numbered in the order you send them, so `@Image1` is the first URL in `reference_image_urls`. Discover describes each uploaded photo first, with a vision model, and writes "the man in the red shirt from @Image1" instead of a bare `@Image1`, which helps the model match photos to positions.

## Send the render

Price it first. The quote needs no API key, and `reference_video_total_duration` is the length of your clip in seconds:

<CodeGroup>
```bash Quote
curl https://api.venice.ai/api/v1/video/quote \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "model": "seedance-2-5-reference-to-video-basic",
    "duration": "auto",
    "aspect_ratio": "adaptive",
    "resolution": "720p",
    "reference_video_total_duration": 13
  }'
```

```bash Render
curl https://api.venice.ai/api/v1/video/queue \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $VENICE_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "model": "seedance-2-5-reference-to-video-basic",
    "prompt": "Generate a video of two performers side by side... (the six parts above)",
    "reference_video_urls": ["https://example.com/clay-clip.mp4"],
    "reference_image_urls": [
      "https://example.com/left-performer.jpg",
      "https://example.com/right-performer.jpg",
      "https://example.com/empty-studio.jpg"
    ],
    "duration": "auto",
    "aspect_ratio": "adaptive",
    "resolution": "720p",
    "bitrate_mode": "high"
  }'
```
</CodeGroup>

A 13-second clip costs $2.00 at 480p and $4.49 at 720p at today's prices. `duration: "auto"` and `aspect_ratio: "adaptive"` make the output match the source clip, and `bitrate_mode: "high"` is the sharper encode Discover uses, at the same price. The render runs as a job, about six minutes for Hotel Lobby: poll `/video/retrieve` as the [video generation guide](/guides/media/video-generation#poll-and-download) shows.

## Make the clay clip

Send the trimmed clip to Seedance 2.5 Edit as its reference video, with an image of the clay style you want as the first reference image. Leave out `duration`: the model takes it from the clip.

```json
{
  "model": "seedance-2-5-edit-video-basic",
  "prompt": "Edit @Video1: turn the two men into smooth, seamless clay figures like @Image1, with bald, egg-shaped heads and no faces. The left man's dark suit and the right man's orange polo shirt and dark shorts are gone. Keep every movement, every cut, the camera and the music exactly as they are.",
  "reference_video_urls": ["https://example.com/source-clip.mp4"],
  "reference_image_urls": ["https://example.com/smooth-clay-style.jpg"],
  "resolution": "480p",
  "bitrate_mode": "high"
}
```

A 15-second clay pass costs $2.31 at 480p. Make the clay once per clip, then reuse it for every render.

## What goes wrong, and the fix

These come from building Discover's templates. Each failure happened in a real render:

- **Name every garment.** Hotel Lobby's right performer kept his orange polo shirt against the orange backdrop until the clay prompt named it: "no fabric and no orange garment remains."
- **Give long hair its own paragraph.** "An egg-shaped bald head with no hair" still left long, swinging hair in every shot. A separate paragraph, "no hair on the clay heads and no strands swinging with the turns", removed it. Hair is what turned a man into a long-haired woman.
- **Make clay figures adult-shaped.** The render copies the clay figure's outline whatever the prompt says. A cat's outline gave a man a tail, a dog's gave people dog ears, and a child-proportioned figure made a man stocky. Ask for "the proportions of a slim adult person, about seven and a half heads tall."
- **Use smooth clay, never a jointed mannequin.** Ball joints and knee discs survive into the render as stepped wrists and stump-like sleeves.
- **Fix a figure the clay pass keeps with a second pass,** not more tries. Run Edit again on the clay output, converting only that figure "into a clay figure exactly like the grey clay figure already in @Video1."
- **Keep body words out of the prompt.** Words like "bare midriffs" or "body outline" can trip the content check with a `422` that returns in under two seconds, every time. Name garments plainly instead.
- **Match the frame rate.** Seedance rejects clips that average under 23.8 or over 60 frames per second, and an output runs about a third of a second short when the input isn't 8n + 1 frames.
- **Keep franchise characters out.** A clip with franchise characters was refused on every part and every wording. Use another clip.

## Recipes by category

### Star in it

One person replaces the performer. Send their full-body photo as `@Image1`, and say in Subjects that they "keep their own anatomy, proportions and visual style from their photo." Grand Hotel Walk adds a second slot for an outfit: the photo of a garment, which the prompt dresses the person in.

### Bring a friend

Two or three people, told apart by position. Hotel Lobby's rule, that the left one is always on the left, after every cut and when they turn around, is what keeps faces from swapping. Name each person's position in every shot of Events.

### Pets

The slot takes anything: a pet, a person or a character. Clay figures get adult human proportions whatever the clip shows, and the prompt adds that "an animal stands upright on its hind legs, as tall as the figure it replaces." Templates where paws or hands come close to the camera stay on Seedance 2.5 Edit, which keeps the original shapes.
